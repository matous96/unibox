Unibox vs Chocolatey

Chocolatey is a proven command-line package manager, popular with admins and developers. Unibox doesn’t replace it – it uses Chocolatey as one of its sources. It offers Chocolatey’s apps in a graphical store alongside WinGet and Scoop, so you can install them without typing commands.

Which one to choose

Each one fits a different job.

Unibox is a good fit if you…

  • don’t want to type commands and prefer to click
  • want to pick from WinGet, Chocolatey and Scoop in one place
  • want to discover apps in a store with recommendations and collections
  • want an overview of every app on your PC, not just packages

Chocolatey is a good fit if you…

  • automate installs with scripts or in CI
  • manage many PCs at a company and want paid support
  • prefer working in a terminal

Questions and answers

Frequently asked questions

Does Unibox replace Chocolatey?

No. Unibox uses Chocolatey as one of its sources. Apps from its repository are in the Unibox catalog alongside apps from WinGet and Scoop, and since version 0.1.10 Chocolatey also serves as a fallback source when WinGet has a broken download link.

Do I need Chocolatey installed?

Not for apps from WinGet – WinGet comes preinstalled with Windows 10 and 11. To install from Chocolatey, yes. In version 0.1.11, Unibox installs it for you in one click using the official install script.

Can I keep using choco commands in the terminal?

Yes. Unibox doesn’t get in the way, and every Chocolatey app comes with a copy-ready choco command you can copy in one click.

Are both tools free?

Unibox is free for everyone and open source under the MIT license. The Chocolatey community edition is free and open source under the Apache 2.0 license; for businesses there are the paid Chocolatey Pro and Chocolatey for Business editions.
